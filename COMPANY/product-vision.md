# Product Vision — Manhattanite

## One-line summary

**Manhattanite is New York's trusted private marketplace.** A vetted, referral-only network for buying, selling, and finding apartments, jobs, and services — without scams, spam, or junk.

## The problem

Craigslist and Facebook Marketplace are the default options for transacting locally. Both are tiring to use. Craigslist is a wall of low-effort listings and scams. Marketplace is full of bots, scrapers, and people who flake. Neither feels safe. Neither feels like New York.

The result: most New Yorkers don't actually transact through them. They ask in WhatsApp groups, post on Instagram stories, or text three friends. The signal is in the social graph. The marketplace is just a search problem nobody's solved.

## The insight

Gens de Confiance proved a different model in France: make trust the product, not the patch. Don't verify after the transaction goes wrong. Verify before anyone gets in. Every member is someone's referral, and every referral carries accountability.

What changes when you do this:

- Bad actors are structurally excluded, not policed
- The network self-polices through social proximity
- Quality compounds because the bar gets higher with each cohort

New York is uniquely suited to this model. Dense, mobile, socially networked, status-conscious, and tired of being scammed. The right 200 people can seed a network that hundreds of thousands eventually want into.

## What Manhattanite is

A private classifieds platform for Manhattan, organized around a two-tier access model.

**Two tiers, one wall.**

- **Account.** Free, no review. Anyone with an email can create one. Accounts let you browse every listing in the network. Accounts cannot post, contact a member, or sponsor.
- **Member.** Application + manual review + approval. Sponsored by an existing member; during the seed phase, George is the sponsor of record. The sponsor is publicly named on the member's profile. Members can post, contact, sponsor.

Account holders see the value. Members capture it. The interaction wall — not the viewing wall — is where trust is enforced. This is the core mechanic.

Four pillars sit on top of that mechanic:

1. **Trust layer.** Every member is vouched for, with the sponsor publicly named. Nobody enters the interactive side of the network anonymously.
2. **Quality layer.** Every listing is curated. Low-effort posts, spam, and junk are removed. The bar is taste, not category rules.
3. **Self-regulation.** Members are accountable to their referrals. The network polices itself through social proximity.
4. **Monetization.** Free for founding members. Pay-per-post later. Never ads.

The feeling we're after: *a well-connected friend showing you the best opportunities in the city.*

## Target user

We're building for one tightly-defined audience first. "Young professionals 22–40" is too vague to write for. These three named personas anchor every product, copy, and growth decision.

### Anna · 28 · The Mover

A young professional in media or tech. Lives in Williamsburg or the Lower East Side. Moves every 18–24 months. Her current apartment search is a mix of StreetEasy, friend texts, and FaceTime tours through a friend-of-a-friend. She has taste, an income, and zero patience for scams.

What she needs from Manhattanite: real apartments from real people. Quality furniture when she moves. A network she can trust.

What she'd say: *"Wait, this is invite-only? How do I get in?"*

### Max · 33 · The Local

Senior person at a startup. Lived in NYC for eight years. Owns the apartment, isn't moving, but constantly hears about job openings, freelance gigs, sublets, things for sale. He's a node, not just a user.

What he needs from Manhattanite: a place to channel the steady stream of intel he already passes around informally. Status from being a known sponsor.

What he'd say: *"I'm bringing my whole group chat into this."*

### Lila · 25 · The Newcomer

Moved to New York 18 months ago. Mid-level role at a creative agency. Doesn't have a deep network yet but is socially active and ambitious about building one. Hyper-aware of where she's getting in and who she's around.

What she needs from Manhattanite: access to a curated network without needing to know everyone already. Apartments, roommates, friends-of-friends.

What she'd say: *"This is where I find my people."*

## What we are not

- Not for bargain hunters or low-end goods
- Not anonymous (every member has a real identity tied to a sponsor)
- Not a Nextdoor (no neighborhood politics, no local griping)
- Not a dating app (Raya already exists)
- Not mass-market (every growth decision is a quality decision)

## Positioning

**For** New Yorkers tired of low-trust marketplaces,
**Manhattanite is** a private, invite-only network
**that** makes buying, selling, and finding the city's best opportunities feel like getting a tip from a well-connected friend,
**because** trust is the product, taste is the moat, and the right network is more valuable than the biggest one.

## Brand promise

Three things every member should feel after their first month:

1. **"I get better stuff here."** Listings are higher quality, more relevant.
2. **"I trust the people."** Real names, real referrals, real accountability.
3. **"I'm in."** Belonging to something selective without it being snobby.

## What success looks like

By end of 2026:

- A working seed-MVP, used for advisor and founding-member conversations
- 50–100 invited founding members across two cohorts
- A sponsor network of 20+ active referrers
- Apartments and furniture categories proven in live use
- A waitlist that's larger than the member list

We are not optimizing for total users in year one. We are optimizing for the quality of the first 200.
