# Voice and Copy — Manhattanite

The verbal identity. How Manhattanite sounds across every surface: marketing, in-product, email, social, support.

Works with `brand-guide.md` (visual identity). George's personal `ABOUT ME/voice-profile.md` and `ABOUT ME/writing-rules.md` apply universally and take precedence over anything here.

---

## Voice in one sentence

**The voice of a well-connected friend, written down.**

Not chatty. Not corporate. Someone who knows the city, knows good things, and is happy to point you to them, without pretending it's a big deal.

## Anchor brand

**Soho House** is the closest reference for tone.

What we take from it:

- Member-aware language ("members," "the network," "our") without it feeling cult-y
- Confident exclusivity that never explains itself
- Editorial paragraphs, not marketing bullets
- Warmth without informality
- The assumption that the reader has taste

What we don't take:

- The slightly-precious house-of-cards naming (no "The Library," "The Salon")
- The Britishisms — we're a New York brand
- Anything that implies hospitality. We're a marketplace, not a club.

## Voice attributes — do / don't

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Confident | Braggy |
| Warm | Chummy |
| Concrete | Abstract |
| Refined | Snobby |
| Direct | Cold |
| Local | Insider-only |
| Plain English | Jargon |
| One clear next step | Buried CTAs |

## Vocabulary

### Words we use

| Term | Where |
|---|---|
| Members | Throughout. Never "users." |
| The network | When referring to the collective |
| Sponsor | The existing member who vouched. Never "referrer." |
| Founding Member | First cohort, capitalized |
| Listing | The thing being posted. Never "post" or "ad." |
| Brought in by | Member-to-member language ("Anna was brought in by Max") |
| Manhattan | When geography matters |
| The city | Casual reference |
| Vouched for | When trust language is needed |
| Apartment, place | Never "property" or "unit" in member-facing copy |

### Words we don't use

| Avoid | Why |
|---|---|
| Community | Overused, generic. Use "the network" or just "members." |
| Platform | Cold and tech-speak. Say "Manhattanite" or "the site." |
| Users | We have members. |
| Engagement, audience, reach | Marketing-speak. |
| Disrupting, revolutionizing | Banned per `writing-rules.md`. |
| Trust-first | Strategy phrase. Stays internal. |
| Curated (as adjective) | Used as buzzword. Show curation, don't claim it. |
| Authentic, real | Demonstrate, don't say. |
| NYC (in formal copy) | "Manhattan" or "the city" reads better. "NYC" is fine casually. |
| Waiting list, waitlist | We don't have a waiting list. People apply for membership. Different framing entirely. |
| Sign up, register, create account | We use "apply for membership." See CTA library. |

### Capitalization

- **Manhattanite** — always capital M, never all-caps, never lowercase
- **Founding Member** — capitalized when referring to the cohort title
- **Member, sponsor, listing** — lowercase unless starting a sentence
- **The Network** — capitalized in marketing copy for weight ("Welcome to The Network"); lowercase in body text

## Spelling and language

**American English throughout.** Manhattanite is a New York brand. Everything here — product UI, marketing, email, social, support — reads in American spelling.

- "organize", "color", "neighborhood", "favorite", "center", "realize", "apologize"
- Quotation marks: straight or curly, but consistent in any one surface. Use the Oxford comma.
- Currency in $ with no decimals on whole-dollar amounts ($5,400, not $5,400.00).
- Dates: "Available July 1" or "1 Jul" in cards, "July 1, 2026" in formal copy. Avoid all-numeric dates in member-facing text.

This overrides George's personal British defaults in `ABOUT ME/writing-rules.md` — for anything that lives under the Manhattanite brand only. His personal voice in non-Manhattanite contexts stays British.

## Tone shifts by context

- **Marketing site** — most polished. Editorial. Reads like a feature article.
- **Inside the product** — quieter. Functional with personality. Less explanation.
- **Member emails** — warm, member-aware. "We" and "our" carry weight here.
- **Notifications** — short, factual, never marketing.
- **Support and moderation** — clear, kind, firm. Don't apologize excessively. Don't soften standards.

## Quality test (from `writing-rules.md`)

Before any piece of customer-facing copy ships, it passes all five:

1. **Specificity.** Real details, names, neighborhoods — not generalizations.
2. **Necessity.** Every sentence earns its place.
3. **Voice.** Sounds like Manhattanite. Could not be any other brand.
4. **Action.** If the reader has to do something, the next step is obvious.
5. **Honesty.** No overclaim. No hype. No empty enthusiasm.

If a piece fails any one, rewrite.
