# GTM Playbook — Manhattanite

How Manhattanite gets from zero to a network worth showing up to. The strategy, tactics, and sequencing for the first 200 members.

Works with `product-vision.md` (who and why), `voice-and-copy.md` (how it sounds), `trust-and-moderation.md` (how the bar is held).

Everything here is a default. Items marked **[ASSUMPTION — confirm or revise]** are calls I made that you should react to.

---

## The GTM principle

**Quality compounds. Quantity decays.**

Every growth decision is a quality decision. The wrong 200 members make the next 200 worse. The right 200 make the next 200 inevitable.

Two consequences:

1. We do not run paid ads. Ever.
2. We do not chase volume metrics. We chase composition.

The right composition for Manhattanite's first 200: socially networked, taste-conscious, Manhattan-resident, age 25–40, professionally established, willing to vouch. People who *are* Anna, Max, and Lila in `product-vision.md`.

## The three phases

| Phase | Members | Focus | Trigger to next |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seed** | 0–20 | Founder-led recruiting. Manual application review. Example data on the site. | All 20 are using the site at least once a week. |
| **Cohort 1** | 20–80 | Sponsor-led growth. First members each bring 3–5 people. | First real listings outnumber example listings. |
| **Cohort 2** | 80–200 | Compound growth via sponsorship + light public surface (referral landing page, press). | Sustained 5+ listings/week, sub-48hr application response time. |

Each phase is a different game. Don't run cohort-1 tactics during seed. Don't run cohort-2 tactics during cohort 1.

## Phase 1: Seed (0–20 members)

### The job to be done

Get 20 high-fit Manhattanites onto the site, posting and browsing, with enough real signal that they tell other people what it is — unprompted.

### Who the first 20 should look like

- **Composition target:** Roughly 8 Annas (movers/young professionals), 8 Maxes (locals/established), 4 Lilas (newcomers).
- **Geographic spread:** Mostly Manhattan, with 2–3 Brooklyn/Queens members who anchor in Manhattan socially.
- **Profession spread:** Not all tech. Mix media, finance, creative, restaurants, design.
- **Behavioral filter:** Already informally sharing listings/intel in WhatsApp groups or Instagram stories. They're nodes whether or not they know it.

**[ASSUMPTION — confirm the 8/8/4 split. Alternative: skew more to Max (locals) because they sponsor more.]**

### How George finds them

In rough order of yield:

1. **First-degree network.** George's own friends and acquaintances who fit. Personally invited.
2. **Second-degree warm intros.** First-degree members each suggest 2–3 people they'd vouch for. George reaches out, mentions the connector.
3. **Specific community pulls.** Casa Magazines regulars, neighborhood favorites' staff, design studios, indie media. Visit, talk, hand over a card.
4. **Soft signal channels.** Substack writers and Instagram accounts with a Manhattan-resident readership. Not for promotion: for identifying the right people to invite one-by-one.

**Not in scope at seed:** Paid ads, broad press, referral programs, public waitlist push. The seed phase is private on purpose.

### What George does each week during seed

A repeatable founder routine. Without this, the network won't compound.

| Activity | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 10 personal outreaches | Weekly | Pipeline of conversations |
| 3 in-person meets | Weekly | Trust is built in person at this scale |
| 1 application review pass | Daily | Application response time = brand signal |
| 1 listing seeded by George | Weekly | Site can't feel empty; example data needs refresh |
| 1 captain's-log review | Weekly | What's working, who's bringing intel, what's stuck |
| 1 GTM retro entry | Monthly | Log lessons in `WORK AREAS/Growth/founding-member-acquisition-project/` |

**[ASSUMPTION — confirm cadence. Alternative if ADHD makes this brittle: collapse to two days a week (e.g., Mondays = outreach, Thursdays = meets), which is easier to stick to.]**

### The first conversation

Not a pitch. A demo + an ask.

A loose script:

> *"I'm building a private marketplace for New York. Trust-based, invite-only, modeled on something that's already working in France. You'd be one of the first 20. I want you in because [specific reason]."*

> *"Two questions for you: (1) Does this feel like something you'd use? (2) Who are the three Manhattanites you'd vouch for — the ones whose taste you'd put on the line?"*

The second question is the unlock. It seeds cohort 2 from inside the seed.

### Materials needed for seed phase

- A short personal email template (in `voice-and-copy.md` style, will go in `WORK AREAS/Growth/founding-member-acquisition-project/`)
- A 30-second site walkthrough (screen recording, no narration)
- A live site with example data — already covered by MVP
- A printed card or handwritten note for in-person handovers — **[ASSUMPTION — confirm appetite for this. Mr Porter / Le Labo-style. Optional but extremely on-brand.]**

### Anti-patterns at seed

- Posting on LinkedIn that you're building a thing. Premature.
- Pitching to press. Premature.
- Building a "join waitlist" landing page. Wrong framing for this phase.
- Inviting more than 5 people in a single week. The wrong shape of growth.
- Onboarding someone who'd be a marginal fit "just to keep the funnel going." This is the failure mode.

## Phase 2: Cohort 1 (20–80 members)

### The job to be done

Turn the first 20 into a sponsor engine. Each member becomes a node. The site moves from "example data + a few real listings" to "the listings are the point."

### How growth works in cohort 1

**Sponsorship becomes the primary acquisition channel.** Every existing member can sponsor new applicants. Each sponsorship:

- Is public on the new member's profile ("Brought in by Anna")
- Is counted in admin views (active sponsors visible to George)
- Is a quality signal: a member who sponsors three high-quality applicants gets visibility/social capital inside the network

**[ASSUMPTION — sponsorship is its own implicit incentive (status + access). No external rewards. Alternative: a "sponsor of the month" feature or a credit toward future paid-post fees once we monetize.]**

### Activation expectations per cohort member

Without these signals, a member isn't actually contributing to the network. Watch them.

| Behavior | Target | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Logs in at least monthly | 80%+ | Anything lower means we have lurkers, not members |
| Posts at least one listing within 60 days | 50%+ | Listings are the network's substance |
| Sponsors at least one applicant within 90 days | 40%+ | Without this, growth stalls |

If activation drops below these numbers, the problem isn't the members. The problem is the product.

### What George does each week during cohort 1

The founder routine evolves. Less personal recruiting, more conducting.

| Activity | Cadence | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Application review | Daily | Response time still matters |
| Sponsor outreach | Weekly | Nudge low-sponsorship members. "Anyone come to mind?" |
| Cohort-1 member call | 2x/week | Listening tour. What's missing? What's working? |
| Listing moderation pass | Daily | Quality bar |
| Public storytelling soft-launch | End of cohort 1 | One Substack/Instagram post about the network — case-study style |

### Anti-patterns at cohort 1

- Treating sponsorship as a referral program. It is not. No "earn rewards" framing.
- Letting members invite anyone with no review. Manual approval is the moat.
- Letting members go quiet without a check-in.

## Phase 3: Cohort 2 (80–200 members)

### The job to be done

Move from founder-led growth to network-led growth. Add a public surface that lets the right people self-identify, without turning Manhattanite into something it isn't.

### Public surface (added in this phase only)

A simple marketing site that:

- Explains what Manhattanite is, in `voice-and-copy.md` voice
- Shows example listings (with members redacted) as social proof
- Offers two paths: "I have an invite" → activation, "Apply for membership" → application form
- Drops the explicit waitlist framing — it's "apply," not "join the queue"

**[ASSUMPTION — confirm: the public site goes live with cohort 2, not before. Going earlier breaks the trust signal.]**

### Press posture

Selective. Specific publications, specific writers, ones who already cover Manhattan-as-culture. Examples:

- Curbed (real estate angle)
- The Cut / NYMag (lifestyle angle)
- The New Yorker (only if the story is right)
- Air Mail (audience match)
- Substack: Feed Me, Blackbird Spyplane, similar tastemakers

**Not in scope:** TechCrunch, Product Hunt, paid PR. Manhattanite is a New York story, not a tech story.

### Monetization introduction

End of cohort 2 / early cohort 3 is when pay-per-post is introduced.

- Members continue to post freely up to a cap (e.g., 1 free listing/month)
- Additional listings: small fee (e.g., $25/listing)
- Premium "featured" listing slots: separate fee
- Always free to browse, free to sponsor, free to be a member

**[ASSUMPTION — confirm pay-per-post is the right monetization. Alternative: annual membership dues, like a club. Probably worth a separate strategic conversation when we get here.]**

### Anti-patterns at cohort 2

- Loosening the membership bar to chase growth. The trust layer is the product.
- Letting the public surface dominate the brand. Members are the brand, the site is the artifact.
- Going paid before the network is sticky enough. Free until it can't be.

## Cross-phase: what holds across all three

### Channel posture

- **Email** — primary member channel. Outlook for George, Resend for product.
- **Instagram** — primary public channel from cohort 2 onward. Editorial, no growth-hacks. Save Stories for member moments, Grid for the network's POV.
- **Substack** — possible founder voice, optional. Decide when George has bandwidth.
- **Word of mouth** — the actual channel, always.
- **No Twitter/X.** Wrong audience, wrong tone.
- **No TikTok.** Same.

**[ASSUMPTION — confirm Instagram-first. Alternative: skip social entirely and let the product be the marketing.]**

### Brand promises that double as growth levers

| Promise | Growth effect |
|---|---|
| Sponsor is publicly named | Sponsor status creates pull for new applicants |
| Application is personally reviewed | Reviewed applicants feel selected and tell others |
| Listings are curated | Quality differentiation is the share-with-a-friend reason |
| Network is small on purpose | Scarcity = aspiration |

The product and the GTM are the same artifact. We don't have a separate growth motion.

### Metrics that matter

In rough priority order:

1. **Active members** (logged in within 30 days)
2. **Listings per active member per month**
3. **Application response time** (target: under 72 hours, average under 48)
4. **Sponsor-to-applicant ratio** (how many sponsors actively bringing people)
5. **Listing approval rate** (proxy for quality of the funnel)
6. **Member retention at 90 days**

### Metrics that don't

- Pageviews, sessions, time-on-site
- Anything from a vanity-metrics dashboard
- Email open rate (we'll send to a small list of high-intent people; opens are not the question)
- Application volume (without composition)

## When to break the rules

These rules exist because we don't have the volume to support generic growth tactics. If we get to cohort 3 and any of the following are true, this playbook gets rewritten:

- 5%+ of applicants are coming in via public channels — the public surface is doing real work
- Sponsorship rates exceed 40% across all members for 3 consecutive months — the network is self-sustaining
- A specific channel is delivering disproportionate quality (e.g., 80% of best members come from one source) — invest there

Don't break rules before then.
