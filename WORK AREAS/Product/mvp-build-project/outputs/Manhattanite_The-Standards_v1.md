## The standards.

**What Manhattanite expects, and what you can expect from us.**

Manhattanite works because of who's on it. Not the software, not the listings — the people. Everything below exists to keep that true.

Read it once. None of it should surprise you.

### Who's here

Every member is a real person, under their real name, living in Manhattan or anchored to it. Applications are read by a person rather than scored by a system, and we approve on judgment.

We decline anonymous applications and pseudonyms. We also decline people who are here to prospect rather than take part — agents working leads, recruiters, agencies. Nothing personal. It's a different kind of place.

### Sponsorship

Most members are brought in by someone already here.

If you sponsor someone, your name sits on their profile permanently, and you answer for how they behave in their first ninety days. That's the whole mechanic. It isn't a formality and it isn't free.

Sponsor people you'd introduce to a friend.

You can hold three sponsorships in review at once. You can't sponsor anonymously, and you can't withdraw a sponsorship once it's approved.

### Listings

A listing should be something you can credibly claim: your apartment, your furniture, your work.

Every listing needs three things.

- **Your own photographs, of the actual thing.** Not stock images, not the manufacturer's shots, not staging photos from a rental site.
- **A real price.** Not "make offer." Not "message me."
- **A description in your own words,** specific enough to be useful. What's good about it, and what isn't.

Listings go into review before they appear. We aim to look within a day. If something needs changing we send it back with a note rather than rewriting it — it's your listing, in your words.

We return or remove listings that use someone else's photographs, recycle a description from elsewhere, misrepresent condition or price, duplicate something already posted, or use the network to route people to a business off it.

### Apartments

Two rules beyond the above.

**No broker listings.** Members post their own places: a sublet, a room, or a direct rental from the owner or leaseholder. Brokers can't post as a service.

**No discriminatory language, anywhere in a listing.** New York's fair-housing protections are wider than the federal ones and they cover source of income. So: no "professionals only," no "no kids," no "no vouchers," no "no Section 8" — and nothing that gestures at the same thing more politely. Listings like this are removed. Posting one again costs you your membership.

If you see a listing that crosses this line, write to info@manhattanite.com. We would rather hear it from you than not hear it.

### Contacting members

A message arrives in a member's inbox with your name and neighborhood on it. Use it to say who you are and what you're interested in.

Don't use it to pitch, to recruit, or to sell something other than what's in the listing.

### What ends a membership

Most problems get a private email and a chance to put them right. These don't:

- Lying about who you are
- Fraud, harassment, threats, or hate
- Using the network to harvest contacts for a business

Repeated listing violations get you to the same place, more slowly.

Removal is final. Your listings are archived and your account closes within seven days. Your sponsor is told. We don't post about it.

### What we don't do

Manhattanite isn't part of any deal between members. We don't take payment, hold money, inspect what's being sold, or referee disagreements. If something goes wrong between two members, it's between the two of you — the same as it would be if you'd met over dinner.

What we do is decide who's here. That's the product.

### Reaching us

**info@manhattanite.com.** A person reads it.

## Three flags before this goes live

**1. The age is contradictory, and this document makes it visible.**
`trust-and-moderation.md` sets the bar at 22+ ("legal contracting age in NY for transactions"). The live Terms of Use at `/terms` says 18. One of them is wrong. I've left the age out of the draft entirely rather than pick. Worth settling with your lawyer friend, since the answer is partly legal.

**2. This document is doing legal work, not just tone work.**
`legal-and-policy.md` names two operational requirements under the fair-housing question: a clear non-discrimination policy, and a complaint mechanism. The apartments section above is both. That makes it the highest-stakes paragraph in the draft and the one most worth a lawyer's eye. The complaint route is an email address because that is what actually exists — there's no report control in the product, and promising one would be a dead control.

**3. Two rules in here are still marked as assumptions internally.**
The broker ban and "removal is final" both carry **[ASSUMPTION — confirm]** in `trust-and-moderation.md`. Publishing them to members converts them from working defaults into stated policy. Both read right to me, but they should be decisions rather than drift.
