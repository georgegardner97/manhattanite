# Manhattanite — Steal Sheet v1 (Phase 1)

**Primary reference: In Common With** (incommonwith.com) — George's pick, 2026-07-17. A Manhattan lighting and furniture studio (showroom at 383 Broadway), which makes it an unusually good fit: editorial, photography-led, warm-paper palette, hairlines everywhere, and local. The brand-guide reference set (Mr. Porter, Soho House, Le Labo) all rhyme with it; ICW is the concrete, living example we design against.

**The rule for every steal:** we take the *structure*, never the skin. Their rounded grotesque becomes Inter; their serif accents become Instrument Serif; their oxblood-on-cream becomes ink-and-park-on-bone; their photography style informs our Phase 4 photo rules.

**One honest caveat before the list.** ICW's design leans on flawless, art-directed photography. Manhattanite's photos come from members. The steals below work because the *grid and hairlines* carry the page even when a photo is mediocre — but this is why the photography rules in Phase 4 (and gentle upload guidance for members) matter more for us than for them. Design for the B-grade photo, and the A-grade photo becomes a gift.

## The twelve steals

**1. Full-bleed photographic hero with chrome overlaid.**
Nav links and wordmark sit *on* the image in light type — no white bar above the photo. One statement line and one quiet CTA anchored bottom-left.
→ **Landing page.** This is the single biggest change: the landing stops being "a page with an image on it" and becomes "an image that is the page."

**2. The label-left editorial grid.**
Every section: a full-width hairline rule, a small label in the left column ("Collections", "Lately", "Newsletter"), content in the remaining ~75%. The label column stays empty below the label — bought whitespace, not wasted.
→ **Every screen.** This becomes Manhattanite's master layout. Our current pages center everything down a single column; the ICW grid is what will make ours feel composed instead of stacked.

**3. Hairline rules as section grammar.**
Sections open with a 1px rule spanning the page. No background changes, no cards, no boxes.
→ **Sitewide.** We already believe this ("hairlines, not boxes") — ICW shows how far it can go.

**4. Serif names floated over category imagery.**
Their collections carousel: large portrait images, collection name set in serif, white, centered on the image. Nothing else.
→ **PARKED (George's call, 2026-07-17).** Two category tiles advertise the narrow launch focus too loudly — "curated" reads as "sparse". No category tiles anywhere for now; the landing shows listings only, and breadth reads implicitly from the mix. Revisit when there are 4+ categories worth showing. The pattern itself (serif over imagery) stays available for hero and future use.

**5. The dated editorial card ("Lately" pattern).**
Two-up cards: small date above its own hairline, image, then title, then a quiet "Read More."
→ **THE LISTING CARD.** This is the flagship steal. Ours becomes: neighborhood + date above a hairline, photo, serif title with price, one line of description, "View listing." It gives every listing the weight of a journal entry — exactly the "editorial entries, not classifieds" instruction in the brand guide.

**6. The statement block.**
A full editorial section spent on one large piece of set text ("Subscribe to our newsletter to stay in the know…") with a single input beside a boxed button. No imagery.
→ **The membership/apply moment on the landing page** and the email-capture pattern generally. Ours says something like "A private marketplace for the people who define New York." Confidence = spending a whole section on one sentence.

**7. Boxed, hairline form controls — the only boxes on the site.**
Input and Sign Up button: 1px border boxes, generous padding. Everything else is naked text and rules.
→ **This solves our biggest UX finding.** The audit flagged that Manhattanite's primary actions look like disabled labels. ICW's answer: buttons ARE boxed — but boxes are *reserved exclusively* for actions, which is what makes them read as pressable. Adopt sitewide: primary = hairline box (ink border, park text or inverse on hover), secondary = arrow link (see steal 9).

**8. The editorial footer as a destination.**
Wordmark top-left; contact, addresses and hours as set columns; link columns under their own hairlines; newsletter repeated; legal line at the bottom. It reads like a magazine's masthead page.
→ **Sitewide footer.** Ours is currently minimal. Addresses ground them in the city — we do the same with East Village / "made in Manhattan" identity, contact, and the terms/privacy links.

**9. The arrow link (→) as secondary action.**
"→ Explore the Collection", arrows for carousel paging. A direction, not a button.
→ **Secondary actions everywhere:** "→ View listing", "→ Browse the network", back links, pagination.

**10. Detail page: anchor rail + statement line.**
Product pages open full-bleed with a tiny left-edge anchor nav (Overview / Description / Tech Specs…) and one serif statement line over the image.
→ **Listing detail.** Full-bleed lead photo with title overlaid; the metadata stack (beds, tags, neighborhood, available) becomes the anchor-railed content below. For long apartment listings the rail is genuinely useful, not decorative.

**11. Accent as text color, not decoration.**
Their maroon appears as *text* — footer links, section copy — never as fills, badges or banners.
→ **This is how park green enters.** Not buttons, not chips: park as the color of footer text, labels, or the hairline under an active nav item. (Feeds the Phase 2 accent decision: park-as-text vs no accent. Brick stays reserved.)

**12. Photography carries warmth; the UI stays neutral.**
Their cream/charcoal chrome is almost colorless — all the warmth comes from the images.
→ **Confirms our palette strategy.** Bone + ink stay quiet; member photos provide the color. Phase 4 photo treatment: warm, natural, interior-led, no filters — their look, our rules.

## What we deliberately do NOT take

- **The dark charcoal bottom bar** in the shared screenshots is Mobbin's frame, not ICW's site. Ignore it.
- **Their rounded grotesque type.** Inter (body/UI) + Instrument Serif (display) stay — this was locked yesterday and the serif just went live. The serif-vs-paid-serif call remains a Phase 2 decision, judged inside this new structure.
- **Carousel-first content.** ICW sells six collections; carousels suit them. Manhattanite is a feed of listings — we keep vertical scroll, stealing only the card anatomy and section grammar.
- **Mega-footer newsletter duplication** — one newsletter/apply block is enough at our size.
