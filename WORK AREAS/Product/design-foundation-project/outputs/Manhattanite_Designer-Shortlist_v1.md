# Designer shortlist and budget (v1)

**Dated 2026-08-17.** Prepared for the design decision taken on 2026-08-13 (§6 of the strategy session): the current design reads "too AI", the open design calls are frozen, and a professional gets engaged. This document gives you the candidates, the money, and the exact scope to buy.

Everything about studios below is from their own sites, checked today. Costs are published market ranges, not quotes. Read them as calibration, not as prices anyone has offered you.

---

## The recommendation, up front

**Buy identity plus art direction plus three key screens as Figma files. Do not buy implementation.**

That single split is the difference between a $30k engagement and an $8–15k one. Most studios price a website as design *and* build, and the build is the expensive half. You already have the build capability sitting in the repo, and Claude Code has implemented every screen on the site so far. So the deliverable you want is flat artwork and a spec, handed over, and then implemented in the existing Next.js codebase the same way the last four design phases were.

Say this in the first email. It changes who can afford to take you seriously, and it changes what they quote.

---

## What you're actually buying

Scope it as four things, in this order:

1. **Identity.** Wordmark, typeface selection and licensing, colour, the rules that hold them together. Replaces the frozen serif and accent decisions.
2. **Art direction.** The photography brief and treatment. This is the piece that most cheaply kills the "too AI" verdict, because generated-looking design is usually generic-image design.
3. **Three key screens in Figma.** Landing, listing card and browse grid, listing detail. Nothing else. The other twenty screens follow the system.
4. **A short handover spec.** Type scale, spacing, colour tokens, states. Enough for implementation without them in the room.

Deliberately out: the full screen set, motion, the email templates (already restyled in Phase 5), any build work.

**Watch-out:** ask for the typeface *licence* cost as a separate line. A studio can specify a face that costs $600 a year and not mention it.

## Budget calibration

Published 2026 ranges for branding work, by tier ([8GNC pricing breakdown](https://8gnc.io/blog/how-much-does-branding-cost)):

| Tier | Range | Typically includes |
|---|---|---|
| Freelance designer | $500–$5,000 | Logo plus basic identity |
| Boutique studio | $5,000–$30,000 | Identity system: mark, type, colour, usage |
| Studio engagement | $15,000–$80,000 | Strategy plus identity plus website |
| National agency | $80,000–$300,000+ | Full rebrand with research phases |
| Retainer | $3,000–$10,000/month | Ongoing brand operations |

My read for your specific scope, with implementation stripped out:

- **A strong independent designer: $6,000–15,000.** This is almost certainly where you land, and it is not a compromise. A good independent with the right taste beats a mid-tier studio for a brand this size, because you get the principal doing the work rather than a junior executing their direction.
- **A small studio: $20,000–45,000.** Realistic if Manhattanite raises, or if you decide the identity is the investment that unlocks everything else.
- **A studio with luxury-brand clients (RoAndCo, Order): $50,000 floor, often much more.** Out of range for now. Contact them for the referral, not the quote.
- **The named studios below sit at the top of that band or above it.** Treat them as taste references and as a benchmark for what good looks like, whether or not you can buy them this year.

One thing worth knowing: pricing spreads this wide because "branding" means five different jobs. Two quotes are only comparable if the scope list is identical. Send the same four-item scope above to everyone.

## The shortlist

### 1. Practical People: the closest fit on subject matter

East Village, New York. Hospitality specialists, describing their clients as "boutique hotels that want to look like themselves, not like everyone else", which is close to a description of your problem. Named work includes Wythe Hotel, Luminara by Ritz-Carlton, Jasper Hotel and The Castle. Identity, naming, typography, environmental design, menus, launch campaigns.

**Why them:** they build brands for places people belong to. That's the register Manhattanite is reaching for, and it's the register a generic product designer will miss.

**Watch-out:** their site emphasises identity and environmental work, not digital product. Ask directly whether they do screen design, and be ready for them to take the identity and art direction while someone else does the screens.

**Bonus:** they are in your neighbourhood. That matters for the kind of in-person working relationship you want.

[practicalpeople.us](https://practicalpeople.us/design-for/hospitality/)

### 2. RoAndCo: the best fit on taste, almost certainly the wrong price

New York and Los Angeles, founded by Roanne Adams. Offers brand strategy, identity, websites, e-commerce, art direction, content strategy and publication design. Adams also founded Romance Journal, so the editorial instinct is real rather than claimed.

**Why them:** they do identity and digital under one roof, and the editorial-lifestyle register is exactly the Mr Porter and Le Labo territory in your steal sheet. On taste, nobody on this list fits Manhattanite better.

**The price problem (revised 2026-08-17).** Their homepage shows mid-size consumer brands. Their about page shows the actual client base: **Gucci, Kate Spade, Altuzarra, NARS, Bobbi Brown, Clinique, Google, Amazon Fashion, Vogue and i-D.** That is a luxury fashion and beauty studio serving brands owned by LVMH and Estee Lauder, not a studio that takes $10k projects.

Estimate, and it is inference rather than a quote: **$50,000 as a realistic floor to open a project, $75,000–150,000 for the full scope.** Even the stripped-back four-item version, with implementation removed, likely lands **$35,000–75,000**. The arithmetic: US branding agencies average $100–149/hour on Clutch, a studio with that client list in New York sits above the top of that band, and the scope is realistically 150–300 hours of studio time.

**Contact them anyway, and here's the real reason.** Not because they'll say yes. A studio at that tier declines small projects constantly and often does it by pointing you at someone right — a former designer now independent, or a smaller studio they rate. That referral is worth more to you than the quote. Ask the question directly: *"I suspect we're below your minimum. If so, who would you send me to?"*

[roandcostudio.com](https://roandcostudio.com) · [about page with the full client list](https://www.roandcostudio.com/about/)

### 3. Rudy: small, hands-on, and does digital product

Brooklyn (475 Kent Avenue). Founded by Michael Freimuth, who ran Franklyn for around 15 years before it wound down. Describes itself as "intentionally small and decidedly hands-on", with everyone working across every project. Services span verbal and visual identity, design systems, websites and digital product, plus fractional creative direction on retainer. Clients named include The New York Times, The Atlantic, Sotheby's, Audible and Away.

**Why them:** the combination you need is rare — identity taste plus digital product work that's actually on their site, plus a studio small enough that the founder is on your project. The Sotheby's and NYT work is the right kind of restrained.

**Watch-out:** that client list prices accordingly. But the "fractional creative direction on retainer" line is interesting for you specifically. A few thousand a month of senior direction, with implementation staying in your hands, could beat a one-off project.

[ru-dy.com](https://ru-dy.com/about)

### 4. Order: the benchmark

Brooklyn and Miami, founded by Jesse Reed and Hamish Smyth. Identity, brand systems, motion, web and digital, typeface design, publication design, signage. Clients include MoMA, Apple, Herman Miller, Knoll, Kickstarter and The Frick Collection. They're also the people behind the reissued NASA and NYCTA standards manuals.

**Why them:** "we do everything for a reason, never for decoration" is the antidote to a design that reads generated. Their systems rigour is what your brand guide is reaching for.

**Watch-out:** almost certainly out of budget. Include them anyway as the calibration point, and because a rejection email sometimes comes with a recommendation attached.

[order.design](https://order.design)

### 5. Gretel: cultural brands, strategy-forward

Brooklyn, 197 Clifton Place. Strategy and design, framed around "clarity of purpose and clarity of communication". Recent work includes the Philadelphia Museum of Art, LOVB, Finer Sounds, Hija de Sanchez and Hart Bageri.

**Why them:** they build brands for institutions and cultural businesses, where the brand has to carry authority rather than novelty. That maps onto trust-as-the-product better than most consumer studios.

**Watch-out:** no explicit digital product offering on the site. Same question as Practical People.

[gretelny.com](https://www.gretelny.com)

**Also noted:** Franklyn, which would have been an obvious name, has wound down after 14 years. Its two founders each started new studios, and Michael Freimuth's is Rudy, above.

## Where to find the independent tier

This is the tier you'll most likely buy from, and it isn't on the studio sites above.

**Do not use Upwork, Toptal, Fiverr or Twine.** The search results are full of them. The work on those platforms is priced and produced for a different problem, and it will produce exactly the generic output you already rejected.

Look here instead:

- **Are.na.** Search channels around editorial design, membership brands, hospitality identity. The people assembling those channels are usually the people you want, and their profiles link out.
- **Instagram, followed backwards.** Find three brands whose design you'd steal from (your steal sheet already names them). Their designer is usually credited or tagged. Message them directly.
- **Studio alumni.** Every studio above has designers who left and now work independently. LinkedIn "past company" search on the five names finds them, and they carry the studio's craft at a third of the price.
- **Cole.** She has worked with Zero Bond. That is precisely the intersection of members-club and New York taste you're aiming at, and one text is cheaper than all of the above.

**How to filter fast:** ignore the logos on the portfolio and look at the *photography*. Art direction is the skill that's hardest to fake and the one your site most needs. If their case studies all use beautiful images, ask whether they directed the shoot or received the assets.

**Missing from the package:** the one-page brief itself, which needs the audience answer. See the sequencing note below.

## The five questions for a first call

1. What would you do with the four-item scope, and what would you quote for it?
2. Would you hand over Figma and a spec and let our side implement, or does that break how you work?
3. Who does the work — you, or someone on your team?
4. What does the photography plan look like on a project with no budget for a shoot yet?
5. What's your view on what's wrong with the current site? *(The tell. Anyone who only talks about the logo is missing the point. Anyone who mentions the imagery has understood it.)*

Ask about fees at the end, and ask for a range rather than a number, the same way the Cole call prep framed it.

## The sequencing problem, stated plainly

A designer's first question will be who this is for, and §2 of the strategy session is still open. Plutocratic consumer versus young professional, and older-and-settled and 25-to-40 point at different typography, different photography, different everything.

You cannot brief this properly until that's decided, and the decision was already scheduled for post-Newport with the friend-interview data in hand.

**This doesn't block starting.** It blocks *briefing*. So:

- **Now:** approach studios, get ranges, see who's available and who's interested. None of that needs the audience answer.
- **Post-Newport:** decide the audience, write the one-page brief, and engage.

If a candidate asks who it's for before you've decided, tell them you're resolving it in two weeks with user interviews. Any good designer will respect that more than a confident wrong answer.
