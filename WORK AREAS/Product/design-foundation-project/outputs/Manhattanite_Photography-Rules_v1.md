# Photography rules (v1) — Phase 4 deliverable

**Dated 2026-08-01.** The photo half of the brand lock. Two audiences: (1) us, choosing brand/marketing imagery; (2) members, whose listing photos are most of the pixels anyone sees. The governing insight from the steal sheet: In Common With's design leans on flawless art-directed photography; ours can't. **Design for the B-grade photo, and the A-grade photo becomes a gift.** The grid and hairlines carry the page; these rules keep photos from fighting them.

---

## 1. Brand photography (hero, marketing, OG, email)

**What we show.** Real Manhattan, at street and apartment scale: brownstone exteriors, lived-in interiors with actual books on actual shelves, doorways, buzzers, neighborhood signs, the park. Recognizable Manhattan, never generic city. Detail over panorama — a stoop beats a skyline.

**What we never show.** Stock photography of any kind. Smiling-at-camera lifestyle shots. Empire State/skyline clichés. Heavy filters, Instagram-amber warmth, vignettes, HDR. People we don't have permission to photograph.

**Treatment.** Natural color, slight grain acceptable, slightly cool over warm. No text on photos except the wordmark/statement line where the design system places it (hero, OG card). Crop tight when in doubt.

**Technical floor.** Hero and full-bleed images: ≥2400px on the long edge (the current hero at 1400px is soft on retina — the standing `TODO(phase-4)`; replacing it is the one open photo task). Licensed or owned only; log the license next to the asset in `public/`.

**Where color lives.** The chrome stays quiet (park dark outside, bone light inside); photographs provide all the warmth. If a page feels cold, fix the photo, not the palette.

## 2. Member listing photos — the standards we moderate against

These extend the listing standards in `trust-and-moderation.md`. Moderation sends photos back for the poster to redo (never platform-edits — same rule as copy).

**Ask of every poster (surface this as gentle guidance on the post form, later slice):**

- Daylight, curtains open, lights on. No flash-in-a-dark-room shots.
- The thing itself, in its space. For furniture: the whole piece plus one honest detail/flaw shot. For apartments: the main room from a corner, the kitchen, the bathroom, the view if it's a selling point.
- Hold the phone level. Vertical for detail shots is fine; the lead photo works best horizontal.
- No filters, no borders, no collages, no text or emoji on images, no screenshots of other listings.
- Nothing identifying third parties: no faces without consent, no visible mail/documents, no building addresses in frame for apartments (the listing copy handles location at neighborhood level).

**Decline triggers (photo-specific):** stock or scraped images (reverse-search when suspicious — a scraped photo is an identity red flag, not just a quality one); photos that materially misrepresent condition; fair-housing-sensitive staging signals in apartment shots (e.g. religious/family imagery used as targeting — see the fair-housing checklist).

**The bar, in one line:** every photo should look like it was taken *for a friend who asked*, not for an ad.

## 3. The lead photo rule

The first photo is the listing card on browse and the full-width lead on detail — it does 90% of the work. Moderation may reorder photos so the strongest leads (allowed — ordering isn't content). If no photo clears the bar for a lead, the listing goes back to the poster before approval.

## 4. Avatars and people

Profile photos: optional, plain, recognizable — a face, natural light. No sunglasses-and-distance shots, no logos, no pets-as-avatar. (Decision 2026-06-08 stands: photo on the profile page only, no inline avatar thumbnails next to bylines.)
