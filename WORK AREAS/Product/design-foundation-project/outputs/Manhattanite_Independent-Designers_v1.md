# Independent designers: names, method and terms (v1)

**Dated 2026-08-17.** Companion to `Manhattanite_Designer-Shortlist_v1.md`, which covered studios. This one covers the tier you'll most likely actually buy from: individuals and two-person practices.

Every name below was verified by fetching the person's own site or a credited project page. Where something couldn't be confirmed it says so. Nobody here is included on reputation alone.

---

## First, an honest word about the East Village

Almost no independent brand designers keep studios in the East Village. Rents pushed that population to Brooklyn a decade ago, and the ones still in Manhattan are in Tribeca and Chelsea. The single East Village design outfit found in two days of looking is Practical People, and they're a studio, not an independent.

So the useful filter isn't where their desk is. It's **whose work already lives in your neighbourhood.** A designer in Williamsburg who branded a bar on Ludlow Street understands your customer better than someone on East 7th who does pharmaceutical packaging. Everyone below is a subway ride from you, and the ones at the top have downtown credits.

## The five to contact

### 1. James Anderson — the best fit on price, register and neighbourhood

Independent, New York, largely self-taught, early-to-mid career. Credited on **Apollo Bagels**, **Café Triste**, **Psychic Wines** including their *Visions* magazine, **Kalei** at 159 Bowery, and **Tuesday Tastings**.

**Why him first:** that list is downtown New York's actual cultural texture, which is the thing your site is missing. He works the small-brand end where a $6–15k project is a real project rather than a rounding error. And the magazine work matters, because editorial layout skill is what separates a site that looks designed from a site that looks templated.

**Caveat, stated plainly:** his own site (jamesanderson.studio) wouldn't load through automated fetching, twice. The location and independent status come from an It's Nice That profile, not from him. Open the site in your browser before emailing. Instagram given as @resortstyleliving.

[It's Nice That profile](https://www.itsnicethat.com/articles/james-anderson-graphic-design-discover-090425) · [Fonts In Use credits](https://fontsinuse.com/designers/10152/james-anderson)

### 2. Triboro — David Heasty and Stefanie Weigler

A genuine two-person duo at 150 North 5th Street, Williamsburg. Identity, publication design, environmental graphics and digital, for publishing, art, fashion, music and cultural institutions.

Hospitality credits: **Tigre** (a cocktail lounge on the Lower East Side), **Jupiter** at Rockefeller Center, **Sauvage**, **Narcissa**, **The Golden Hour**. Wider roster includes MoMA, The New York Times, Nike, Marc Jacobs and the Prince Estate.

**Why them:** typography-led, editorially literate, and they do digital as well as identity, which means no handover seam between your identity and your screens. Both principals work on everything because there are only two of them.

**Watch-out:** the Nike and MoMA end of that roster suggests they may price above $15k. Ask early, and lead with the reduced scope.

Contact: david@triborodesign.com · [triborodesign.com](https://triborodesign.com/info)

### 3. Javas Lehn Studio — the only person with a members-club credit

New York, 20 West 20th Street. Founded by Javas Lehn after MoMA and Wolff Olins. Roughly 1–10 people.

The relevant credit: **The Twenty Two**, a Mayfair hotel and private members' club. Nobody else found in this search has branded a members' club. He also did The Robey.

**Why him:** he has solved your exact positioning problem before, which is the difference between a designer who interprets your brief and one who improves it.

**Watch-out:** Four Seasons and Chanel are also on the roster, so expect upper-end pricing. He may still be worth the paid discovery step alone (see terms below) even if the full project goes elsewhere.

[javaslehnstudio.com](https://javaslehnstudio.com/)

### 4. Elana Schlenker — likely the most affordable of the group

Independent, working from the Pencil Factory in Greenpoint. Branding and art direction, interactive work, packaging, print. Publisher and designer of *Gratuitous Type*.

Clients: A24, Aperture, Phaidon, Anthology Editions, The New York Times, Walker Art Center, Columbia GSAPP, ICP, Figma, The Verge.

**Why her:** arts, publishing and nonprofit-weighted client lists usually mean rates calibrated to arts, publishing and nonprofit budgets. The typographic register is precisely the restrained editorial thing your steal sheet is chasing, and A24 is about as good a taste signal as exists.

**Watch-out:** no hospitality or membership work. She'd be interpreting your category rather than recognising it.

[elanaschlenker.com](https://elanaschlenker.com/about)

### 5. Anna Polonsky — Polonsky & Friends

Brooklyn studio, food and hospitality focused. Credits include **Santo Taco** in SoHo and **Bar Miller**.

**Why her:** on sector, this is arguably the closest fit of anyone on the list. Hospitality is the whole practice, not a category within it.

**Caveat:** their site blocks automated fetching, so team size, current location and contact details are unverified. Everything here comes from a Dezeen piece and an interview. Look at the site yourself before deciding.

## The method that finds the rest

The names above took two days. The technique below finds more, and it's the one skill worth keeping.

**Don't search for designers. Find identities you like and read the credit.**

1. **[Fonts In Use](https://fontsinuse.com)** — filter by the Food/Beverage and Retail topics. Credits the individual designer, not just the studio. The single best tool here.
2. **[The Brand Identity](https://www.the-brandidentity.com)** — curated, credits every project. Also runs a jobs board that is **free to post**, with a freelance filter and live New York listings.
3. **[Brand New](https://www.underconsideration.com/brandnew/)** — every identity review names who did it.
4. **[SiteInspire](https://www.siteinspire.com)** — for the screens half. Credits the studio on web work.
5. **[Are.na](https://www.are.na)** — alive and healthy. Profiles are public without login and carry a personal website link, which is your bridge to an email. Mine channels rather than searching users; the user search is weak.
6. **Instagram** — still where these people live. Search plain language ("brand designer New York"), not hashtags. Open the Places tab on bars and shops whose branding you admire and read the tagged posts for credits. Business accounts expose an email button.

Build a list of twenty-five identities you actually admire, pull the credited name, go to their own site, email them. Free, no platform in the way, highest possible taste signal.

## How to structure the engagement

This matters as much as who you pick.

**Open with paid discovery, not the full project.** Standard practice is 5–10% of the anticipated project value, or a flat $500–$2,500, delivering a short written diagnosis of the brand problem and how they'd approach it. Ask explicitly that the fee is **credited against the full engagement** if you proceed within two to six weeks, which is the normal term.

Why this is the right first move for you specifically: it's a real trial of working together for a fraction of the commitment, it tells you whether they understood Manhattanite or just liked the brief, and it means you can afford to run it with two candidates and pick the better one.

**Never ask for free concepts, a test logo, or a pitch round.** AIGA's position paper explicitly discourages spec work, and asking for it silently filters out exactly the designers you're trying to reach. The correct version of that instinct is to ask for prior work plus a written statement of approach, which is what paid discovery buys.

**Terms to expect:**

| Item | Normal range |
|---|---|
| Deposit | 50% for a project under $5k with a new client; 25–33% above that |
| Milestones | 30% signing / 40% midpoint / 30% delivery, or a plain 50/50 |
| Payment window | Net 15 is a reasonable ask from an independent |
| Late fee | 1.5–2% per month, standard, expect it in the contract |
| Kill fee | Scaled to completion. Get it written down. |
| Timeline | 8–12 weeks for identity plus three key screens |

That timeline assumes one decision-maker, which you are, and 48–72 hour feedback turnaround from you. Client slowness is the usual cause of overrun, and Newport plus the audience decision means your first two weeks are already slow. Say so up front rather than discovering it later.

**Contract:** the [AIGA Standard Form of Agreement for Design Services](https://www.aiga.org/resources/aiga-standard-form-of-agreement-for-design-services) is free and modular. It's a fair starting point that neither party has to argue about.
