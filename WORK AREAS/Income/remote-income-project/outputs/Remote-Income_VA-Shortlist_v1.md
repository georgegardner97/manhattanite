# Part-time VA setup and other remote options, v1

The one-line strategy: agencies for steadiness, Upwork for rate, your own network for both. Set up all three doors in the same week; whichever opens first pays first.

## Door 1: agencies (steadiest, lowest rate)

They find the clients, you do the hours. Contractor or employee, so income is predictable once matched. Expect 2 to 4 weeks from application to first paid hour.

1. **Belay** (belaysolutions.com). US-based VA/executive assistant placements for professional clients. Contractor, roughly $18 to 24/hr, part-time friendly. The standard first application.
2. **Time Etc** (timeetc.com). Flexible hours, US arm pays roughly $17 to 24/hr, wants some prior admin or ops experience. Botanical Brothers covers that.
3. **Boldly** (boldly.com). Premium tier, W-2 employment, $25+/hr, but they want 7+ years of EA/admin experience. Apply only if you can frame your ops history that way; otherwise park it.
4. **Fancy Hands.** Per-task pay, low rates. Skip except as pocket money while waiting on the others.

How to apply: each has a careers/apply page, a skills assessment, and a video or phone interview. Position yourself as ops support, not general admin (see the blurb below).

## Door 2: Upwork (set your own rate, faster if it lands)

US-based VAs and ops assistants charge $25 to 50/hr on Upwork. First job can land within days if the proposals are specific. Setup:

1. Profile headline: "Ops assistant for founders" beats "Virtual assistant." You're selling founder-to-founder context, which most VAs can't.
2. Rate: start $30/hr. Don't go lower; US-based + founder experience justifies it.
3. First proposals: filter for US clients, 10 to 20 hrs/week, ops/admin listings. Write 5 short custom proposals a day for a week. Ignore anything paying under $20.
4. Take a small first job fast to get a review on the board, then hold your rate.

Contra and PeoplePerHour are the same play with smaller pools. Do Upwork first, add Contra if it's slow.

## Door 3: your own network (highest rate, fastest when it works)

One message: "I'm taking on one or two founders for 10 hours a week of ops support: inbox, scheduling, vendors, the loose ends. $35/hr." Send it to founder contacts, the NANM ecosystem, and anywhere else it fits. One yes beats a month of agency onboarding, and a $35/hr retainer at 10 hrs/week is $1,400/month on its own.

## The application blurb (paste and adapt)

American spelling, for US clients:

> I run operations for my own startup and previously ran ops for a delivery business I helped scale, so I know a founder's week from the inside: the inbox, the vendors, the scheduling, the loose ends that eat focus. I take those off your plate. Tools I use daily: Notion, Airtable, Google Workspace, Todoist, and I'm fast with AI workflows. NYC-based, Eastern hours, reliable.

## More remote options (beyond VA)

1. **Remote customer support, part-time.** $18 to 25/hr, W-2 steady, genuinely low load. Search We Work Remotely, remote.co, and FlexJobs for "part-time support." Slower to land (real hiring process) but the most predictable income on this page.
2. **Community moderation / management.** Brands and startups pay $20 to 30/hr for Discord/forum moderation and community management. Evenings-friendly, low load. Find via the same job boards plus direct asks to products you already use.
3. **Airbnb co-hosting, remote side.** NYC hosts pay per listing per month for someone to handle guest messages and scheduling cleaners. Pure ops, fully remote, recurring. Find hosts through NYC host Facebook groups or people you know who host.
4. **Notion/Airtable micro-builds.** Small fixed-price setups ($300 to 800) for businesses on Upwork. More brain than the rest of this list, but you already live in both tools, and it stacks on the same Upwork profile as the VA work.

## Honest rate comparison

AI training platforms likely beat agency VA work per hour ($30ish vs $20ish) with zero interviews. The VA work's advantage is that it's steady and relationship-based where AI training tasks come and go. Running both covers each one's weakness.
