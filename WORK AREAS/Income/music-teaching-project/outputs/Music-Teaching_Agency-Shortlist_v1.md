# Agency shortlist and week-one actions, v1

The strategy in one line: agencies buy you students and teaching reps now, the private roster is where the real rate lives, and you build both at once.

Agency teacher pay in NYC runs roughly $24 to 59/hr based on current listings (ZipRecruiter, July 2026). Direct private in-home rates in Manhattan run $80 to 150/hr. So every student who comes to you directly is worth 2 to 3x an agency student. Agencies are the bridge, not the destination.

## Agencies worth applying to

**In-home companies (closest match to your offer):**

1. **Hey Joe Guitar** (heyjoeguitar.com). In-home lessons across Manhattan and Brooklyn, all instruments despite the name. Teachers travel to family homes, which is exactly your model. Apply via their contact page.
2. **Musication** (musication.nyc). Brooklyn-based, in-home lessons for kids, guitar and piano focus. Kid-first positioning that matches yours.
3. **Music To Your Home.** NYC in-home and online lessons company, hires working musicians. Verify current openings on their site.
4. **New York Guitar Academy** (nyguitaracademy.com). Private lessons in NYC, Brooklyn and online.

**Schools (steadier hours, more structure):**

5. **NYC Guitar School.** Group classes and privates, hires instructors, locations in Manhattan and Brooklyn.
6. **School of Rock** (Manhattan/Brooklyn locations). Group band programs. Lower hourly but reliable blocks, and their whole model is kids performing real music, so your songwriting angle fits the interview well.

**Online (for the portable months):**

7. **Lessonface.** Marketplace where you set your own rate. Slow to start, but it's the piece that travels to Taos/California with you.

## Week one, in order

1. **Line up two references** who can speak to you working with kids or teaching anything. This is the one thing agencies and parents both ask for.
2. **Apply to Hey Joe Guitar and Musication** (30 minutes each). Position: working bassist/songwriter, East Village, gets beginners to their first real song.
3. **Post Version B of the blurb** in your own circles first: band contacts, NANM crew with kids, your building, and the wider network where it feels right. Free, warm, and where the first student most likely comes from.
4. **Build the one-pager site** from Music-Teaching_One-Pager_v1.md. One afternoon, and you have the copy already.
5. **Decide the guitar question honestly.** You're a bassist. Beginner guitar is well within reach, but spend a week prepping the first-ten-lessons material (chords, simple songs) so the first lesson is smooth. If it doesn't feel right, lead with bass and songwriting only. "The bass teacher" is a sharper niche than "another guitar teacher" anyway.

## Parked for later (don't do these yet)

- Park Slope Parents commercial post (paid; wait until the site is live and you've tested the pitch)
- Liability insurance (roughly $100 to 200/yr; needed before regular in-home lessons, not before applying to agencies, which carry their own)
- Background check certificate (agencies run theirs; get your own once you go direct)
- After-school program applications (the school year is the entry window; check for January openings, otherwise revisit in spring for September 2027)
