# The one-line pitch, reworked (v1)

**Dated 2026-08-01.** Replaces "Raya meets Craigslist" as the opener. Written after the Marwan meeting (2026-07-14) showed what the old line does: it frames Manhattanite as a hype-y consumer startup, invites the "tech companies are lottery tickets" objection, and buries the utility. This doc also serves as the deliverable for Chloe's "Craft a Confident Elevator Pitch" exercise (assigned 2026-07-29).

The test for every line here, from the strategy: **invite-worthy because useful.** The pitch must lead with the problem it solves, not the scene it evokes.

---

## Why "Raya meets Craigslist" fails

Both halves work against us. "Raya" says exclusive-because-cool (the positioning we explicitly rejected) and hands skeptics the startup-hype framing. "Meets" says venture-scale mashup. The listener files it under "app idea" and starts evaluating odds instead of hearing the problem. Marwan never got to the substance — the opener decided the meeting in the first ten seconds.

The fix is not a better mashup. It's leading with the thing everyone already hates.

## The recommended default

> **"It's Craigslist, if everyone on it was vouched for."**

This was already the outreach kit's one-liner, and it survives the Marwan test: it opens with a utility anchor everyone knows, and the twist *is* the trust model. Nothing in it claims cool. The word "vouched" does all the work. **Recommendation: promote this to the only default opener, retire "Raya meets Craigslist" entirely** (the kit's "Craigslist meets Raya" swap-in stays available for the scene-literate only, per the 2026-07-13 guardrails — but it is never the opener with skeptics, professionals, or anyone over 40).

## Variants by listener

- **The skeptic / businessperson (the Marwan case):** "You know how nobody actually uses Craigslist anymore — they ask their group chats? I built the group-chat version of classifieds. Invite-only, every member vouched for by another member. There's a French site that proved the model with two million members."
- **The practical New Yorker:** "A classifieds site for Manhattan where you can't get scammed, because everyone on it was brought in by someone who put their name on it. Apartments and furniture first."
- **The proof-first listener:** "In France there's a site called Gens de Confiance — two million people, and the whole thing runs on one rule: members vouch for new members. Nothing like it exists in New York. I built it."
- **Deliberately small-scale framing (defuses the "tech startup" objection):** "It's not a startup-startup — it's a members' club that happens to be a classifieds site. Costs about ten dollars a month to run. I'm picking the first twenty members by hand."

## The 30-second elevator pitch (Chloe's exercise)

> "Nobody in New York actually uses Craigslist or Facebook Marketplace anymore — they're all scams and bots. So people ask their group chats: anyone know an apartment, anyone selling a couch. The trust lives in the social graph; the marketplaces just never caught up. I've built Manhattanite to fix that. It's a private classifieds site for Manhattan — invite-only, and every member is vouched for by an existing member whose name stays on their profile. So people behave. A French site called Gens de Confiance proved this works — two million members. Nothing like it exists here. It's live, apartments and furniture first, and I'm handpicking the first twenty members now."

Beats, for delivery practice: **pain** (everyone nods) → **insight** (the group chat is the real marketplace) → **product** (one sentence) → **mechanism** (the vouch, the name on the profile) → **proof** (GDC, 2M) → **now** (live, first 20). Roughly 30 seconds spoken at conversational pace. Close with the kit's two questions: "Would you actually use this?" and "Who are the three Manhattanites you'd vouch for?"

## Kill list (unchanged, worth repeating)

Never: community, platform, users, "disrupting," exclusive-because-cool, anything that invites the word "startup" before the word "useful." If the listener's first question is about venture capital, the pitch failed — reset with the small-scale framing above.
