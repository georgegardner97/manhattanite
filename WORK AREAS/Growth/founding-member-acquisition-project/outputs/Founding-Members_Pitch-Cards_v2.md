# Founding members — pitch cards (v2)

**Dated 2026-09-02.** Replaces v1 (2026-07-13). Three things changed: the opener is now the finalised one from `Manhattanite_One-Line-Pitch_v2.md`, every angle line is rebuilt around shared liability rather than a name on a profile, and "sponsor" is gone (the product stopped saying it on 31 August).

The Raya variant is deleted, not moved. It is retired for every listener type.

## The system: spine + swap

Memorise one spine. Only beat 2 changes per person.

1. **Open** — *"It's an invite-only listings site for New Yorkers. A member has to vouch for you to get in — and if you behave badly, they go too."*
2. **Swap** — the angle line for their type. Why *they* should care.
3. **Picture** — *"Think about your group chat. Someone's got a spare room going and you trust it straight away, because you know who you're dealing with."*
4. **Close** — the two questions: *"Would you actually use this?"* / *"Who are the three Manhattanites you'd vouch for?"*

Four beats. Twenty seconds. Everything after that is conversation.

If you forget everything mid-sentence: opener, their motive, the two questions. That is a complete pitch.

## The four motives

Know which one is sitting across from you and you can improvise the angle line and still land it.

| Motive | The person | Types |
|---|---|---|
| **PAIN** | Has suffered the alternative | Mover, Newcomer |
| **PRIDE** | Their judgement about people carries weight | Local, Connector, Tastemaker |
| **SUPPLY** | Has, or hears about, the goods | Landlord, Fixture, Hospitality, Design, Creative |
| **MODEL** | Wants to know why it works | Finance, Tech, Media |

Pain → tell their story back to them. Pride → their judgement is the asset, not their name. Supply → what they have finds a better room. Model → trust built first, marketplace on top.

**Note the shift from v1.** Pride used to be about whose name means something. It is now about whose judgement you'd trust, because that is what the vouch actually is — a bet the member is making with their own membership.

## The cards

### Young professional / mover — PAIN

**Angle:** "You know what it's like trying to find anything here. Scams, flakes, people who don't show. On this, whoever you're dealing with was let in by someone who's now answerable for them."

**Ask:** What are they hunting for or offloading right now? That becomes their first search or first listing.

**Watch for:** They convert on their own story. Ask "what's your worst marketplace story?" and land the opener on top of their answer.

### Established local — PRIDE

**Angle:** "The first ten members set the standard for everyone who comes after. I want people whose judgement about other people I'd trust."

**Ask:** Lean on question 2. They know exactly who they'd vouch for, and their vouches carry.

**Watch for:** Don't oversell. Confidence, not flattery. They join because it's serious, not because you need them.

### Newcomer to the city — PAIN

**Angle:** "This is the network people spend ten years building, and you'd have it on day one — except everyone in it has somebody answering for them."

**Ask:** What are they hunting for right now?

**Watch for:** They may feel they have nothing to offer. Tell them newcomers make the market. They're the buyers and the renters.

### Connector — knows everyone — PRIDE

**Angle:** "You already make introductions for free. Here they count for something, and they cost you something too — which is exactly what makes them worth having."

**Ask:** Skip question 1, go straight to question 2. They'll offer ten names; take three.

**Watch for:** Their vouches are the growth engine, but hold the ceiling even when they hand you a list. And say the liability part out loud to this type specifically — a connector who vouches casually is the single biggest risk to the standard.

### Neighbourhood fixture — shop, cafe, building staff — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "You hear who's moving before their landlord does. Here that's worth something."

**Ask:** Send the person, not the tip. When someone mentions moving out, that's an invite moment.

**Watch for:** This pitch happens across a counter, not over email. Casual and short.

### Apartment supply — landlord, buildings — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "Every enquiry comes from someone a member let in and stays answerable for. Not anonymous strangers — you can see who brought them in."

**Ask:** One vacancy or sublet as a first listing.

**Watch for:** Fair housing. The listing describes the apartment, never the wanted tenant. You approve or decline; you never rewrite. **Never say "likeminded" or anything about the type of person, in this conversation above all** — the standard is about conduct, not about who people are. Full screen in `Fair-Housing_Research_v1.md`.

### Tastemaker — has an audience — PRIDE

**Angle:** "It's small on purpose, and the first ten decide what it becomes. I'd rather you shaped it than heard about it later."

**Ask:** Membership only. Do **not** ask them to post about it. Seed phase is private and a public shout now is the anti-pattern.

**Watch for:** They can smell being wanted for reach. The pitch is being early, not being a channel.

### Creative — artist, musician, designer — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "Artists already run on word of mouth — sublets, gear, spaces. This is the same thing with a search bar."

**Ask:** First listing: the amp, the flat file, the summer sublet.

**Watch for:** Price sensitivity. Lead with "free to join, free to browse" before they have to ask.

### Design and interiors — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "Somewhere the things for sale are actually good, and photographed like it matters. Your pieces deserve a better room."

**Ask:** List one piece. Their photography sets the standard for the whole site.

**Watch for:** Business accounts are out of scope. Invite the person, not the studio. Client-offload pieces park until later.

### Media, writer — MODEL

**Angle:** Tell the long spoken version, then the proof: "There's a French site called Gens de Confiance running on the same single rule. Two million members. Nothing like it here."

**Ask:** "I'm not pitching coverage — that comes much later, if ever. I want you in it as a member."

**Watch for:** Saying "no press yet" makes it more interesting to them, not less. The embargo is part of the pitch.

### Finance, business — MODEL

**Angle:** "It filters the way you already filter everything — through people who've put something of their own at risk on the recommendation."

**Ask:** Question 2, hard. Their vouches tend to be fast and high quality.

**Watch for:** They'll ask about the business model. One line: "Pay-per-post later. Membership free forever." Then move on.

### Tech, startup — MODEL

**Angle:** "Every marketplace bolts trust on at the end — reviews, badges, dispute resolution. I built the trust first and the marketplace on top of it."

**Ask:** Both questions work as written.

**Watch for:** "How does this scale?" is coming. The answer is "slowly, on purpose." Don't get pulled into growth talk. The restraint is the model.

### Other, unclassified

Open, picture, two questions. If the conversation goes well you'll know their motive by the end of it — retype them in the tracker.

## Practice drill (10 minutes)

1. Say the spine out loud three times, until beats 1, 3 and 4 are automatic.
2. Shuffle: pick names off the tracker at random. Say the motive out loud, then the angle line.
3. Before each coffee: 60 seconds. Motive, angle line, tailored ask. Nothing else.
