# Founding members — outreach kit (v1)

**Dated 2026-07-02.** Three tools in one place: the outreach templates, the elevator pitches, and the listing-supply play. All member-facing copy is American spelling per `voice-and-copy.md`.

---

## 1. Outreach templates

These are skeletons, not scripts. The rule from the playbook: every message names a *specific reason* this person, or it doesn't send. If you can't fill the [because] slot, they're not top-20 material yet.

### Text / WhatsApp — first-degree friends (the default)

> Quick one — I've built something and it's live. A private marketplace for New York: invite-only, every member vouched for by another member. Think Craigslist without the chaos. I'm handpicking the first 20 members and you're on my list because [specific reason — their taste / their network / their apartment history]. Can I show you over coffee this week? Ten minutes, then you can tell me I'm crazy.

### Email — warmer or more formal contacts

**Subject:** The first 20

> Hi [name],
>
> I've spent the last few months building Manhattanite — a private marketplace for New York. Invite-only, every member brought in by someone who vouches for them, every listing checked by a person before it goes live. Apartments and furniture to start.
>
> It's live, and I'm choosing the first 20 members by hand. I want you in because [specific reason].
>
> Can I buy you a coffee this week and show you? Ten minutes on my phone. Two questions for you at the end.
>
> George

### Second-degree — came through a vouch

**Subject:** [Connector] gave me your name

> Hi [name],
>
> [Connector] and I were talking about Manhattanite — a private marketplace for New York I've just launched. Invite-only, everyone vouched for by another member. When I asked who they'd put their name behind, you were the first person they said.
>
> I'm handpicking the first 20 members. Coffee this week? Ten minutes and you'll get it.
>
> George

### The two questions (close every conversation with these)

1. "Would you actually use this?"
2. "Who are the three Manhattanites you'd vouch for?"

Question 2 is the engine. Log every name in the tracker's vouched bench.

## 2. Elevator pitches

### The one-liner (lead with this, always)

> **"It's Craigslist, if everyone on it was vouched for."**

Anchor + twist. Everyone knows Craigslist; the twist lands the whole model in one breath.

### The party version (~15 seconds)

> "It's a members-only marketplace for Manhattan — apartments and furniture right now. You get in when a member vouches for you, and their name stays on your profile. So no scams, no spam, no strangers. It's small on purpose."

### The full version (~30 seconds, for people who lean in)

> "There's a site in France called Gens de Confiance — 'people of trust.' Millions of members, and the whole thing runs on one rule: you can't get in unless existing members vouch for you. It made classifieds feel like getting a recommendation at a dinner party. Nothing like it exists in New York, which is insane, because New York runs on exactly that kind of word-of-mouth. So I built it. Apartments and furniture first. The first 20 members are being picked by hand — that's the conversation we're having."

### The skeptic version (when someone says "so it's just another marketplace")

> "Every marketplace bolts trust on at the end — reviews, badges, dispute buttons. We built the trust first and the marketplace on top of it. When your name is publicly attached to the person you brought in, people behave. That's the whole product."

### Vibe lines (sprinkle, don't stack)

- "Small on purpose."
- "Your sponsor's name stays on your profile. People behave."
- "No scams, no spam, no strangers."
- "A recommendation, not a transaction."

**Never say:** community, platform, users, exclusive-because-cool, "disrupting." The brand is invite-worthy because *useful*.

## 3. The listing-supply play

**Principle: poach posters, not listings.** Never repost or scrape someone's ad — the trust bar requires the actual owner posting. "Getting listings" always means recruiting the person who has something to list. Which is convenient, because the invite *is* the product.

### The trigger moments (when people have listings in them)

People don't have listings; life events do. Moving out of the city. Moving in together (duplicate everything). Redecorating. Renovating. Subletting for the summer. Downsizing. Find the moment, and the listing — often several — comes with the person.

### Sources, ranked by fit

1. **Your 38, on joining.** Make "bring one listing" part of the onboarding ritual — like a housewarming gift in reverse. At approval, every new member gets asked: "What's the first thing you'll list?" The activation target is already 50% posting within 60 days; this makes it a social norm from member one.
2. **Phil Lavoie + supers and doormen.** Apartment supply is the harder, more valuable side. Buildings know about vacancies, sublets, and estate situations weeks before anyone else.
3. **Facebook Marketplace and Manhattan Facebook groups.** Scout the sellers whose listings are photographed well and written with care — they're rare and they hate the platform they're on. Personal invite, one by one: "your sofa deserves a better room."
4. **Listings Project.** Weekly letter of apartments and sublets with a creative, high-trust audience — the closest existing thing to our member profile. Posters there are pre-filtered for exactly our sensibility.
5. **AptDeco and Chairish sellers in Manhattan.** Design-conscious by definition; their photos are already up to standard.
6. **Craigslist's good apples.** They exist, buried. Same one-by-one play.
7. **Instagram stories.** Friends posting "selling this" or "anyone need a couch" — be the reply that says "list it properly."
8. **Interior designers and stagers** — they offload client pieces constantly. Flag: business accounts are out of MVP scope, so this means inviting the *person* where they genuinely fit the member profile, not the firm. Park the rest for v2.

### The rules that hold

- The poster becomes a member first (sponsored, approved) — no exceptions, or the wall means nothing.
- The seller's fair-housing screen applies to every apartment listing regardless of who brought it.
- Quality bar over volume, always: the metric is listings per active member, not raw count.
