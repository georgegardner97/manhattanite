# Founding members — pitch cards (v1)

**Dated 2026-07-13.** Companion to `Founding-Members_Outreach-Kit_v1.md`. That file holds the templates and core pitches. This one is what to say to each candidate *type* on the tracker, built to be memorized. Spoken copy is American spelling per `voice-and-copy.md`.

---

## The system: spine + swap

You memorize one spine. Only beat 2 changes per person.

1. **Open** — the one-liner: *"It's Craigslist, if everyone on it was vouched for."*
2. **Swap** — the angle line for their type (cards below). Why *they* should care.
3. **Proof** — one mechanic: *"Your sponsor's name stays on your profile. People behave."*
4. **Close** — the two questions: *"Would you actually use this?"* / *"Who are the three Manhattanites you'd vouch for?"*

Four beats. Fifteen seconds. Everything else is conversation.

## The four motives (if you memorize only one thing)

Every type on the tracker responds to one of four motives. If you know which motive is sitting across from you, you can improvise the angle line and still land it.

| Motive | The person | Types |
|---|---|---|
| **PAIN** | Has suffered the alternative | Mover, Newcomer |
| **PRIDE** | Name and taste carry weight; this makes it visible | Local, Connector, Tastemaker |
| **SUPPLY** | Has, or hears about, the goods | Landlord, Fixture, Hospitality, Design, Creative |
| **MODEL** | Wants to know why it works | Finance, Tech, Media |

Pain → tell their story back to them. Pride → their name matters here. Supply → what they have finds a better room. Model → trust built first, marketplace on top.

## The Raya variant

For one specific New Yorker, swap the opener:

> **"It's Craigslist meets Raya."**

**Who it lands with:** the scene-literate New Yorker. Roughly 25–38, socially dense, creative / media / fashion / music / hospitality, downtown-coded, treats admission-by-vetting as a normal part of life. They either know Raya or know exactly who's on it. On the tracker: Tastemakers, Connectors, Creatives, Hospitality, and the more social Movers.

**Why it works on them:** "Craigslist" names the utility, "Raya" names the door. Four words carry the whole model — a useful thing, in a vetted room, with a standard of person implied. To this crowd it also signals taste level in a way "vouched for" doesn't.

**The read-the-room test:** would they know what Raya is without you explaining it? If yes, use the line. If you'd have to explain Raya, the line is dead on arrival — fall back to "vouched for."

**The chaser (don't skip it):** Raya pulls the pitch toward exclusive-because-cool, which is exactly the positioning we rejected. So the line never travels alone. Follow it immediately with the utility beat:

> "...but the door isn't the point. The point is that everything inside actually works — no scams, no flakes, no strangers."

Someone who joins for the velvet rope lurks for status. Someone who joins for the working marketplace posts. The chaser filters for the second.

**Who it misfires with:** established locals over 40 (may read Raya as frivolous), finance (dating app = unserious), landlords (irrelevant), newcomers (may not get the reference), and media — for them "Raya for Craigslist" is a ready-made headline, and seed phase is no-press. Don't hand them the phrase.

## The cards

### Young professional / mover — PAIN

**Angle:** "You know what it's like finding anything here — an apartment, a sofa, a subletter. Scams, flakes, noise. Imagine every listing came from someone vouched for by name."

**Ask:** What are they hunting for or offloading right now? Apartment, furniture, anything — that becomes their first search or first listing.

**Watch for:** They convert on their own story. Ask "what's your worst marketplace story — Craigslist, Facebook, anywhere?" then land the one-liner on top of their answer.

### Established local — PRIDE

**Angle:** "The first 20 members set the tone for everything after. I want people whose name means something here."

**Ask:** Lean on question 2. They know exactly who they'd vouch for, and their vouches carry.

**Watch for:** Don't oversell. Confidence, not flattery. They join because it's serious, not because you need them.

### Newcomer to the city — PAIN

**Angle:** "This is the network people spend ten years building — vouched apartments, furniture, intel — and you get it on day one."

**Ask:** What are they hunting for right now?

**Watch for:** They may feel they have nothing to offer. Tell them: newcomers make the market. They're the buyers and the renters.

### Connector — knows everyone — PRIDE

**Angle:** "Sponsorship is public. Everyone you bring in carries your name. You already make introductions for free — this gives them a home."

**Ask:** Skip question 1, go straight to question 2. They'll offer ten names; take three.

**Watch for:** Their vouches are the growth engine, but hold the 5-invites-a-week ceiling even when they hand you a list.

### Neighbourhood fixture — shop / café / staff — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "You hear about people moving before their landlord does. This is the place where that's worth something."

**Ask:** Send the person, not the tip. When someone mentions moving out, that's an invite moment.

**Watch for:** This pitch happens across a counter, not over email. Keep it casual, keep it short.

### Apartment supply — landlord / buildings — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "Every inquiry comes from someone vouched for by name. No anonymous strangers — you can see who brought them in."

**Ask:** One vacancy or sublet as a first listing.

**Watch for:** Fair housing. The listing describes the apartment, never the wanted tenant. You approve or decline; you never rewrite. (Full screen in `Fair-Housing_Research_v1.md`.)

### Tastemaker — has an audience — PRIDE

**Angle:** "It's small on purpose, and the first 20 decide what it becomes. I'd rather have you shaping it than hearing about it later."

**Ask:** Membership only. Do **not** ask them to post about it — seed phase is private, and a public shout now is the playbook's anti-pattern.

**Watch for:** They can smell being wanted for reach. The pitch is being early, not being a channel.

### Creative — artist / musician / designer — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "Artists already run on word of mouth — sublets, gear, spaces. This just gives it an address."

**Ask:** First listing: the amp, the flat file, the summer sublet.

**Watch for:** Price sensitivity. Lead with "free to join, free to browse" before they have to ask.

### Design & interiors — SUPPLY

**Angle:** "A marketplace where the furniture is actually good and the photos are taken like it matters. Your pieces deserve a better room."

**Ask:** List one piece. Their photography sets the standard for the whole site.

**Watch for:** Business accounts are out of MVP scope. Invite the person, not the studio. Client-offload pieces park until v2.

### Media / writer — MODEL

**Angle:** Tell the full 30-second version — the Gens de Confiance story from the outreach kit. It's a good story and they'll enjoy it told well.

**Ask:** "I'm not pitching coverage — that comes much later, if ever. I want you in it as a member."

**Watch for:** Saying "no press yet" makes it more interesting to them, not less. The embargo is part of the pitch.

### Finance / business — MODEL

**Angle:** "It filters the way you already filter everything — through people who stake their name on the recommendation."

**Ask:** Question 2, hard. Their vouches tend to be fast and high quality.

**Watch for:** They'll ask about the business model. One line: "Pay-per-post later. Membership free forever." Then move on.

### Tech / startup — MODEL

**Angle:** The skeptic version: "Every marketplace bolts trust on at the end. We built the trust first and the marketplace on top."

**Ask:** Both questions work as written.

**Watch for:** "How does this scale?" is coming. The answer: "Slowly, on purpose." Don't get pulled into growth talk — the restraint is the model.

### Other / unclassified

Default to the party version from the outreach kit, then close with the two questions. If the conversation goes well, you'll know their motive by the end of it — retype them in the tracker.

## Practice drill (10 minutes)

1. Say the spine out loud three times, until beats 1, 3, and 4 are automatic.
2. Shuffle: pick names off the tracker at random. Say the motive out loud (pain / pride / supply / model), then the angle line.
3. Before each coffee: 60 seconds. Motive, angle line, tailored ask. Nothing else.

If you forget everything mid-conversation: one-liner, their motive, the two questions. That's a complete pitch.
