# Cole Spike call — discussion points

*Prep for George. One page. The call has two jobs: get her thinking on five real problems, and work out whether she's the right hire. Her answers to the first do the second for you.*

A framing tip before the questions: don't hand her a blank slate ("how would you market this?"). Describe your current plan in two sentences, then ask her to attack it. You learn far more from how someone criticises a plan than from how they improvise one. Your two sentences: *"Invite-only marketplace for Manhattan, modelled on Gens de Confiance in France. No paid ads ever — the plan is founder-led seeding of 20 members, then sponsor-led growth where every member is publicly vouched for by the person who brought them in."*

---

## 1. The chicken-and-egg problem

**Your position going in:** the site is live and demo-ready with seeded example listings, so it never looks empty. The plan is 20 hand-picked members before anything public. The open question isn't *whether* to start small — it's how to make 20 people feel like the start of something rather than an empty room.

**Ask her:**
- "You've launched things where the value was the other members — Zero Bond sold belonging before anyone had been inside. How do you build wanting-in before there's much to want in *to*?"
- "Which side would you seed first — people posting listings, or people looking? And what's the smallest version of this that feels alive?"
- "What did the founding-member group actually do for Zero Bond's launch — and what would you steal from that playbook here?"

## 2. Standing out from Listings Project, Girls Who Sublet, Ohana

**Your position going in:** they curate the *listings*. Nobody curates the *people* on both sides. Listings Project is an open newsletter — anyone can subscribe; vetting is one-way. Girls Who Sublet is an Instagram DM pipeline. Ohana is a legal-compliance play for hosts. None has named sponsorship, member accountability, or more than one category. That's the difference on paper. The question is whether it's a difference a normal person can *feel*.

**Ask her:**
- "How do you make 'trust' legible in marketing without literally saying 'trust us' — which nobody believes?"
- "Do we even compete with free Instagram accounts for the same moment, or should the wedge be the thing they don't do — furniture, and eventually jobs?"
- "If you had to pitch the difference in one sentence at a dinner party, what would you say?"

## 3. Is subletting too saturated to cold-start with?

**Worth knowing:** your launch categories are apartments + furniture — sublets are inside the apartments category, not the whole bet. The counter-view to "saturated": saturation of *low-trust* supply is exactly the gap. But it's a fair challenge, and furniture is less contested ground.

**Ask her:** "If you could only lead the story with one category, which one — and why?" Her reasoning here tells you a lot about how she thinks about wedges.

## 4. Is 25–40 even the right market?

Your new question, and it's a sharp one. The current personas skew 25–40. The counter-case: 45+ established New Yorkers — UES, West Village — have more money, more to lose, and trust is a genuine dealbreaker for them, not a nice-to-have. Gens de Confiance's real base in France is families, not twentysomethings. On the other hand, older members are harder to reach digitally, sublet less, and may sponsor more slowly. Zero Bond's mix skews older-money — she'll have a real view on this.

**Ask her:** "Who actually pays for trust in this city — the 28-year-old who sublets every 18 months, or the 50-year-old who's been burned once and never again? And can you build for one without repelling the other?"

## 5. The money question

Don't ask "how much do you cost" — ask how she works. It's the same information, framed as respect for her craft:

> *"Before we go further — how do you typically structure engagements? Retainer, project-based, advisory? And what does a typical engagement look like for something at my stage?"*

Then be honest about where you are, which sets the budget conversation up without apologising:

> *"I'll be straight with you — this is pre-revenue and I'm deliberately keeping it lean until the network proves itself. If the full engagement isn't the right fit yet, is there a scoped-down version — a few advisory hours a month — that could work as a starting point?"*

Asking about structure first and stage second is the professional version. She'll respect it, and the advisory option gives you both a cheap way to test the fit.

## Extras, if there's time

- **Timing check:** "Is it too early to hire marketing at all when growth is meant to be founder-led? What would you actually *do* in the first 90 days?" If she can't name concrete deliverables, that's your answer on the hire.
- **Test the no-ads posture:** the playbook rules out paid ads and tech press (it's a New York story, not a tech story — Curbed and The Cut, not TechCrunch). See if she agrees. If she reaches for performance marketing, she's the wrong fit.
- **Her network is the asset:** "Who else should I be talking to?" — worth asking even if you don't hire her.
- **Before the call:** send her the site link. Her unprompted reaction to the real thing is worth more than an hour of hypotheticals.
