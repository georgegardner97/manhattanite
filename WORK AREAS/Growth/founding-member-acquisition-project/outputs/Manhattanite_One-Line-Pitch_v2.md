# The pitch — finalised (v2)

**Dated 2026-09-02. This is the decision.** Replaces `Manhattanite_One-Line-Pitch_v1.md` (2026-08-01), which recommended an opener but was never chosen and sat on the wave-one blocker list for a month. Written with George in the session; the mechanism at the centre of it is his wording, not a rewrite of the old material.

Two descriptions, because they do two different jobs. **Spoken wins on punch. Written wins on dignity.** Don't make one line do both.

---

## The spoken description

**Short — ten seconds. The default. This is the one to memorise.**

> "It's an invite-only listings site for New Yorkers. A member has to vouch for you to get in — and if you behave badly, they go too."

The surprise is the last five words. Let them land; don't talk over them.

**Long — twenty-five seconds. Only if they lean in.**

> "It's an invite-only listings site for New Yorkers. Apartments, furniture, whatever you need. A member has to vouch for you to get in, and they stay responsible for you: break the rules and you're both out. So people are careful about who they bring in.
>
> Think about your group chat. Someone posts that they've got a spare room going, and you trust it straight away, because you know exactly who you're dealing with. That's the feeling I'm building."

## The written description

The invitation. The reply when someone asks what it is. The paragraph you paste.

> Manhattanite is an invite-only listings site for New Yorkers. Apartments, furniture, things worth passing on. The same stuff you'd find on any classifieds site.
>
> The difference is who's on it. A member has to vouch for you before you can join, and they stay responsible for you afterwards. If you break the rules, you're both removed. So people are careful and considered about who they bring in.
>
> Think about your group chat. Someone mentions they've got a spare room going and you trust it straight away, because you know exactly who you're dealing with. That's what I'm building, at the scale of a city.
>
> It's small on purpose. I'm choosing the first ten members by hand, and I'd like you to be one of them.

---

## Why these, in four lines

1. **The mechanism moved from a label to a consequence.** "Their name stays on your profile" describes a feature. "If you break the rules, they go too" describes what happens to you. Everything else in the pitch is downstream of that swap.
2. **Craigslist is the comparison, never the self-description.** Radio H-P's founder calls his site "an honourable and trusted network"; the press calls it "a posh Craigslist". Gens de Confiance calls itself "a trustworthy ad site"; everyone else calls it classifieds. Both let other people make the comparison. When someone says "oh, like a posh Craigslist" — agree, warmly. Never say it first.
3. **The written version leads with the familiar and turns on the second paragraph.** "The same stuff you'd find on any classifieds site" is the setup. "The difference is who's on it" is the turn.
4. **It ends on the invitation, not on an ask.** "I'd like you to be one of them" is the last thing they read.

## Rules of use

- **Never volunteer Craigslist, Raya, or the word "startup".** If the first question back is about venture capital, the pitch has failed — reset with: "It's not a startup-startup. It costs about ten dollars a month to run and I'm picking the first ten members by hand."
- **Don't name jobs or services.** Apartments and furniture are what's live. "Things worth passing on" covers the rest without promising a category that doesn't exist. Add the words when the thing exists.
- **Say "the member who vouched for you", never "your voucher"** in anything written down. Fine out loud, wrong in copy (decided 2026-08-31 — it reads like a coupon).
- **Ten, not twenty.** The recut says ten invitations in wave one. Say the same number everywhere; the first person you invite will ask.
- **Spelling.** British in anything you write and send as yourself. American everywhere on the site. The only word where it shows is *honourable*, which is one more reason it isn't in either version.
- **Held in reserve, never volunteered:** "Craigslist with a door policy." Only for a listener who has gone blank.

## The close (unchanged, still the best part)

> "Would you actually use this?"
>
> "Who are the three Manhattanites you'd vouch for?"

Ask the second one every time, even when the conversation has gone badly. Hesitation about putting their name to someone is the product working.

## Retired

- **"It's Craigslist, if everyone on it was vouched for."** Good sentence, wrong job — it made a comparison the self-description, and it points at furniture and apartments at the moment those stopped being the wedge.
- **"Raya meets Craigslist" / "Craigslist meets Raya."** Retired 1 August, and the retirement stands. Not a scene-literate swap-in, not a fallback, not for anyone.
- **"Sponsor" in any spoken or written pitch.** The word is gone from the product; it goes from the pitch too.

## What this pitch now depends on

**The cascade removal is a promise the product has not yet made.** The decision inventory records that the vouching model has no rule for what happens to the people a removed member vouched for. As of today the pitch says "you're both out" — so that rule has to exist, in writing, before the first invitation goes out on 7 September. It is a two-line policy and it is now the strongest sentence in the pitch, so it is worth making true rather than softening.

Related and still open from the same list: no probation before a new member can vouch, and no cap on how many people one member may vouch for. Neither blocks the pitch. Both shape whether the promise holds at fifty members.
